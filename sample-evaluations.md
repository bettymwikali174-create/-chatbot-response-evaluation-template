## Evaluation 1: Explaining a concept

**Prompt:** What is the difference between a budget and a forecast?

**Response A:**
A budget is a plan for how money will be earned and spent over a set period, and it acts as a target. A forecast is an estimate of what is likely to happen, based on current results and trends. A budget usually stays fixed for the year, while a forecast is updated as new information comes in. For example, a shop may budget for 1,000,000 shillings in sales, then forecast 900,000 after a slow quarter.

**Response B:**
A budget and a forecast are the same thing. Both are lists of expected income for the year, so you only need one of them.

**Scores for Response A:**
- Accuracy 5: The definitions and the example are correct.
- Relevance 5: Directly answers the question.
- Completeness 5: Gives both definitions, the key difference and an example.
- Clarity 5: Short, plain and well ordered.

**Scores for Response B:**
- Accuracy 1: Says the two are the same, which is wrong.
- Relevance 3: On topic, but does not answer the question
