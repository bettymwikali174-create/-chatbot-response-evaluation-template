# Scoring Rubric

Score each response from 1 (very poor) to 5 (excellent) on the four criteria below. Score each criterion separately, then decide which response is better overall.

## Accuracy

Is the information factually correct?

- 5: Everything stated is correct and nothing is misleading.
- 3: Mostly correct, with a small error or an unclear claim.
- 1: Contains major errors or invented information.

## Relevance

Does the response answer the question that was asked, and follow the instructions in the prompt?

- 5: Fully on topic and follows every instruction, such as length or format.
- 3: Mostly on topic, but includes unneeded content or ignores a minor instruction.
- 1: Off topic or ignores the main instruction.

## Completeness

Does the response cover everything the prompt needs?

- 5: Covers all key points with enough detail.
- 3: Covers the main point but leaves out something important.
- 1: Leaves out most of what was asked.

## Clarity

Is the response easy to read and understand?

- 5: Clear, well organised and in plain language.
- 3: Understandable, but wordy, repetitive or hard to follow in places.
- 1: Confusing, badly organised or full of errors that block understanding.

## Final assessment

After scoring, write a short final assessment that includes:

1. Which response is better, or whether they are about equal.
2. The one or two main reasons, referring to the criteria above.
3. Any serious issue, such as a factual error or an ignored instruction, that should be flagged.

## Notes for consistency

- Judge the response, not the writing style you personally prefer.
- If a response has a serious factual error, do not give it a high accuracy score, even if it is well written.
- If a prompt is unclear or you are unsure how to score something, flag it for review instead of guessing.
